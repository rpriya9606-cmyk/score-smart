Romeo and Juliet

William Shakespeare

📌 Important Questions
1. Mention any two images to which Romeo compares Juliet in the poem "Romeo and Juliet".
Romeo compares Juliet to "a rich jewel in an Ethiope's ear" and "a snowy dove among crows".
2. Mention any two expressions that Juliet uses to glorify Romeo's charm in the poem "Romeo and Juliet".
Juliet uses expressions like "day in night" and "whiter than snow on a raven's back".
3. Who are compared to 'a snowy dove' and 'crows' respectively, in the poem "Romeo and Juliet"?
Juliet is a snowy dove, others are crows.
4. Mention any two qualities of night according to Juliet in "Romeo and Juliet".
Night is gentle and loving. It helps Juliet meet Romeo secretly.
5. What does all the world start doing and stop doing, according to Juliet, if Romeo becomes a star?
People will love the night and stop worshipping the sun.
📘 Additional Questions
1. Mention any two similes that Romeo uses to describe the beauty of Juliet.
"Rich jewel in an Ethiope's ear" and "snowy dove among crows".
2. What does Romeo intend to do after the measure is done?
He will watch Juliet and touch her hand.
3. Whose hand is referred to as 'rude'? When would this hand be blessed?
Romeo's hand. It will be blessed when he touches Juliet's hand.
4. Who, according to Juliet, would all the world be in love with at night? Why?
The world will love night because Romeo will shine like a star.
5. When and how does Juliet expect Romeo to come?
At night, like a star.
6. What would people forget to worship, according to Juliet? Why?
They will forget the sun because Romeo will make night beautiful.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
Romeo and Juliet is one of Shakespeare's most romantic tragedies. The given extract expresses the feelings of Romeo and Juliet when they see each other for the first time.

When Romeo sees Juliet on the dance floor, he is deeply fascinated by her beauty. He feels that her beauty makes the torches seem brighter and compares her to a rich jewel in an Ethiope's ear. He also compares her to a snowy dove among crows.

He decides to wait until the dance ends and then go to her and touch her hand, believing it will bless his rough hand. He realizes that he has never seen anyone as beautiful as Juliet before that night.

On the other hand, Juliet is also attracted towards Romeo and describes his charm. She calls him "day in night" and compares him to new snow on a raven's back. She asks the night to take Romeo to the sky and turn him into little stars so that he will make the sky beautiful and charming.

She feels that the whole world will fall in love with the night and stop worshipping the bright sun because of Romeo's beauty.

Too Dear!

Leo Tolstoy

📌 Important Questions
1. Mention any two things that make Monaco a toy kingdom?
Monaco is a very small country with around 7000 people. It also has a very small army of only 60 men.
2. Name the two countries that bordered Monaco.
Monaco is bordered by France and Italy along the Mediterranean Sea.
3. Mention any two supporting staff/officials of the King of Monaco.
The king had ministers, judges, prosecutors, jury members, cooks, servants, and guards as staff.
4. Name any two commodities that were taxed in Monaco.
Tobacco, spirits, and wine were taxed in Monaco.
5. Which commodities did the Prince of Monaco draw revenue from?
The Prince of Monaco got revenue from gaming houses and also from taxes on tobacco, spirits, and wine.
6. Who were the main beneficiaries of the gaming house in Monaco?
The main beneficiaries were the Prince of Monaco and his government, whether people won or lost.
7. Give any two reasons that made German Sovereigns forbid gaming houses.
Gaming houses caused people to lose money and fall into financial ruin. Some people even committed suicide in despair.
8. Name any two things that the Prince of Monaco did on a smaller scale as a king.
He conducted coronations, gave rewards, sentenced criminals, and pardoned offenders on a small scale.
9. Mention any two professionals who tried the murder case in the most judicial manner.
Judges, barristers, prosecutors, and jury members took part in the trial.
10. What were the works assigned to the guard when the criminal was kept in prison?
The guard had to watch the prisoner and also bring food from the palace kitchen.
11. How much pension was fixed for the criminal? How much money did he receive in advance?
A pension of 600 francs per year was fixed. He received one-third of it in advance.
12. What did the criminal do after receiving his annuity in advance?
He bought a piece of land, started market gardening, and lived comfortably.
📘 Additional Questions
13. What was the request made to the French government and the reply?
The ministers asked for a guillotine and executioner. France replied it would cost 16,000 francs.
14. Why was help from Italy not considered suitable?
Italy's help would increase taxes on the citizens, so it was rejected.
15. Why was the King's army unable to help in the execution?
The soldiers were not trained to perform executions.
16. What was the new expenditure faced by the King? What was his fear?
The new expense was 600 francs per year for the prisoner's maintenance. The king feared the cost would increase over many years.
17. Meaning of proverb 'You can't earn stone palaces by honest labour'?
It means honest work alone may not bring enough wealth to live luxuriously.
18. Why was the king not willing to pay for the French guillotine and executioner?
Because the French government demanded a very high cost.
19. Why did the council believe that the king of Italy would come up with a cheaper solution?
They believed Italy would offer a cheaper solution than France.
20. What measure did the council finally take to get rid of their prisoner?
They gave the prisoner a pension of 600 francs per year, and he accepted it.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
'Too Dear' is a story written by Russian writer Leo Tolstoy. Monaco was a small kingdom. It had a king, ministers, a small army of sixty soldiers and seven thousand population. Tax was collected from tobacco, wine, spirits and a poll tax, but the special revenue came from the gaming houses.

All was well in Monaco, until a man committed a murder and was sentenced to death. Monaco did not have a guillotine or an executioner to execute the murderer. They sought help from France and Italy. While France demanded 16,000 francs, Italy demanded 12,000 francs. It was too dear for Monaco.

At last, they asked the soldiers to cut off the murderer's head, but they refused as they were not trained for it. So, the death sentence was converted into life imprisonment and a guard was appointed. The man was fed from the palace kitchen. The yearly cost was 600 francs, which was heavy for Monaco.

To cut costs, they dismissed the guard and thought the prisoner would escape, but he did not. When asked, he replied that he could not go anywhere as his character was ruined. So, he was released and offered a pension of 600 francs per year, receiving one-third in advance. The prisoner started market gardening and lived comfortably collecting 600 francs every year from the government.

On Children

Khalil Gibran

📌 Important Questions
1. What are the two reasons given by the Prophet to say, "Your children are not your children"?
Children are the sons and daughters of Life's longing for itself. They also have their own thoughts.
2. What may parents give and may not give to their children, according to the Prophet?
Parents may give love to their children but not their thoughts.
3. What may parents not give to their children? Why?
Parents may not give their thoughts because children have their own thoughts.
4. According to the Prophet, what may parents house and what may they not?
Parents may house children's bodies but not their souls.
5. Who are the arrows and who sends them forward in the poem?
Children are arrows and parents are bows.
6. What do 'bows' and 'arrows' refer to in the poem?
Bows are parents and arrows are children.
7. According to the Prophet, who bends the bow and why?
God is the Archer who bends the bow so arrows go far.
8. Who sees the mark upon the path of the infinite? Why does he bend the bow with all his might?
The Archer sees the mark and bends the bow to send arrows far.
📘 Additional Questions
9. Why should parents try to become like children?
Parents should become like children because children are innocent and pure.
10. Who is referred to as 'Archer'? Where does he see the mark?
God is the Archer. He sees the mark upon the path of infinity.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
The poem "On Children" is written by Kahlil Gibran. In this poem, the poet gives valuable advice to parents. He says that children are not the possessions of their parents. They are the sons and daughters of Life's longing for itself. They come through their parents but not from them. Though they are with their parents, they do not belong to them.

Parents can give their children love, but not their thoughts, because children have their own thoughts. They can house their bodies, but not their souls, because their souls dwell in the house of tomorrow, which parents cannot visit even in their dreams. Parents may try to be like their children, but should not make their children like them, because life moves forward and never turns back.

The poet compares parents to bows and children to living arrows. God, the Archer, guides and loves both parents and children.

Everything I Need to Know I Learned In the Forest

Vandana Shiva

📌 Important Questions
1. What were the parents of Vandana Shiva?
Vandana Shiva's father was a forest conservator, and her mother was a farmer.
2. What is Chipko? Women from which region participated in it?
Chipko is a non-violent movement against deforestation. Women from the Garhwal Himalaya region participated in it.
3. According to Vandana Shiva, who knew the value of forests? What did they do to protect them?
The peasant women of the Garhwal Himalaya knew the value of forests. They hugged the trees to stop them from being cut down.
4. What is the real value of forests, according to the women of the Chipko Movement?
The real value of forests is springs and streams, food for cattle, and fuel for homes, not timber.
5. What did Vandana Shiva do while she was on the padayatras of the chipko movement? Or list the activities that Vandana undertook after her involvement with the chipko movement.
She documented deforestation, supported forest activists, and spread the message of the Chipko Movement.
6. What did Vandana Shiva learn from the Chipko Movement?
She learned about biodiversity and biodiversity-based living economies.
7. How did Vandana Shiva and other activists help farmers in the Navdanya Movement?
They helped farmers shift from chemical-based farming to biodiverse ecological farming.
8. What did biodiversity teach Vandana Shiva?
Biodiversity taught her abundance in food production, freedom, and the value of cooperation.
9. Which are the two most popular courses at Vandana Shiva's Earth University?
The two most popular courses are "The A-Z of Organic Farming and Agro-ecology" and "Gandhi and Globalization."
10. Name any two conflicts that Tagore's has written about?
Conflict between greed and compassion and conquest and cooperation.
📘 Additional Questions
11. Which two countries recognised and declared the Rights of Nature?
Ecuador and Bolivia recognised and declared the Rights of Nature.
12. How does the scarcity of water, fodder, and fuel affect women?
Women have to walk longer distances to collect water and firewood, making their work harder.
13. What features of the Chipko Movement does Vandana Shiva highlight?
It was non-violent, led by peasant women, and aimed at protecting forests.
14. How are unity and diversity related to each other?
Diversity without unity leads to conflict, while unity without diversity leads to control.
15. What is the Navdanya Movement? When was it started?
The Navdanya Movement promotes biodiversity conservation and organic farming. It was started in 1987.
16. When and where was the Navdanya Farm started?
Vandana Shiva started the Navdanya Farm in the Doon Valley in 1994.
17. Where and why did Rabindranath Tagore start a learning centre?
He started a learning centre at Shantiniketan, West Bengal, to learn from nature and promote Indian culture.
18. Mention any two things that replaced the "vibrant earth."?
Raw materials and dead matter replaced the vibrant earth.
19. Mention any two conflicts that Vandana Shiva mentioned in her essay.?
Conflict between greed and compassion and violence and harmony.
20. What do the terms "Terra Madre" and "Terra Nullius" mean?
Terra Madre means Mother Earth. Terra Nullius means empty land ready for occupation.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
The lesson "Everything I Need to Know I Learned in the Forest" is written by Vandana Shiva. She was a volunteer for the Chipko Movement, which was against cutting trees in the Himalayan region. Vandana Shiva and others protested by hugging the trees and saved them.

She appreciates Bachni Devi for opposing her own husband, who had obtained a contract to cut trees. She tells us how she learnt about environmentalism from the uneducated women of Garhwal, Himalaya.

She started the Navdanya Farm to educate farmers and save seeds. She used methods of bio-diversity and organic farming. She has started Earth University, which promotes a "Live and let live" culture among all species.

She mentions Tagore's writings, where he says that the forest is the source of beauty and joy and symbolizes the universe. People exploit the earth, but the forest teaches people to enjoy the gifts of nature without exploitation and accumulation.

A Sunny Morning

Serafin and Joaquin Alvarez Quintero

📌 Important Questions
1. What is the name of the book Don Gonzalo reads in the park? Who has written it?
Don Gonzalo reads a book of poems written by Campoamor.
2. Name any two Spanish writers mentioned by Don Gonzalo in the play 'A Sunny Morning'.
Don Gonzalo mentions Espronceda and Zorrilla.
3. In 'A Sunny Morning', how old is Don Gonzalo? Which is his native place?
Don Gonzalo is 70 years old. His native place is Valencia, Spain.
4. To whom do the words 'the gallant lover' and 'the silver maiden' refer to respectively in the play 'A Sunny Morning'?
The gallant lover refers to Don Gonzalo. The silver maiden refers to Dona Laura.
5. Where did Gonzalo meet Zorrilla? How old was he when he went there for the first time, in the play 'A Sunny Morning'?
Gonzalo met Zorrilla in America. He was six years old when he first went there.
6. Name the king and queen mentioned by Gonzalo while referring to Columbus, in the play 'A Sunny Morning'.
Gonzalo mentions King Ferdinand and Queen Isabella.
7. Mention the name of the villa near Valencia. Who lived in it?
The villa was called Maricela. Dona Laura lived there.
📘 Additional Questions
8. When Don Gonzalo prepares to read, he takes out two things from his pocket. Which are they?
He takes out an enormous pair of glasses and a reading glass.
9. What was Dona Laura's original name? By what name was she known in her locality?
Her original name was Laura Llorente. She was known as "The Silver Maiden."
10. Who accompanied Don Gonzalo and Dona Laura, respectively, when they came to the park?
Don Gonzalo was accompanied by Juanito. Dona Laura was accompanied by Petra.
11. What could Gonzalo and Dona Laura show as evidence to prove their skill at hunting?
Gonzalo could show a wild boar's head. Dona Laura could show a tiger's skin.
12. Why is Don Gonzalo ill-tempered at the beginning of the play?
He was annoyed because three priests occupied his usual bench.
13. What makes Dona Laura conclude that Don Gonzalo is an ill-natured man?
He scared away the birds and spoke rudely to her.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
The play "A Sunny Morning" is written by Serafin and Joaquin Alvarez Quintero. It tells the story of Don Gonzalo and Dona Laura, who were true lovers in their youth. However, their love was not successful as they were separated by circumstances.

The play begins on a sunny morning in a park where Dona Laura is feeding breadcrumbs to the pigeons when Don Gonzalo enters. He is a seventy-year-old gentleman, gouty and important, while Laura is a graceful, white-haired lady of about seventy.

Their conversation begins with sarcasm as they accuse each other of disturbing their peace. A pinch of snuff breaks the tension, and they soon begin talking in a friendly manner.

During their conversation, they realize that they were lovers in their youth, but both hide their real identities.

Laura loved Gonzalo, but her parents wanted her to marry a rich merchant whom she disliked. One day, a quarrel took place between Gonzalo and the merchant, and the merchant was badly wounded. Gonzalo fled to Madrid, and they lost contact with each other.

Later, it was believed that Gonzalo had run away to Paris with a ballet dancer, while Laura married after two years.

Even after recognizing each other, they do not reveal the truth. Instead, they create stories about the deaths of their younger selves. Laura pretends to be the friend of the real Laura, and Gonzalo pretends to be the cousin of the dead Gonzalo.

The play ends with the hope of meeting again the next morning.

When You Are Old

W.B. Yeats

📌 Important Questions
1. To whom is the poem "When You Are Old" addressed? What should the person do with the book given by the speaker?
The poem is addressed to the poet's beloved. He asks her to take down the book, read it slowly, and dream of the past.
2. How was the speaker different from other lovers?
Other lovers loved her beauty and grace. The speaker loved her pilgrim soul and the sorrows of her changing face.
3. What features of the lady does the speaker imagine when she becomes old?
The speaker imagines her as an old woman with grey hair, full of sleep, and nodding by the fire.
4. What did the speaker ask his beloved to dream of?
He asks her to dream of the soft look her eyes once had and their deep shadows.
📘 Additional Questions
5. What did the speaker love in his beloved?
The speaker loved her pilgrim soul and the sorrows of her changing face.
6. In 'When You Are Old', how does the poet want his beloved to read the book of poems?
In 'When You Are Old', the poet wants his beloved to read the book of poems slowly and thoughtfully.
7. Why did most admirers love the poet's beloved?
Most admirers loved the poet's beloved because of her beauty, grace, and happy moments.
8. Who is the 'one man' mentioned in the poem? What did he love?
The one man is the speaker himself. He loved her pilgrim soul and the sorrows of her changing face.
9. Mention any two aspects that the speaker loved in his beloved.
The speaker loved her pilgrim soul and the sorrows of her changing face.
10. Who fled and hid his face? Where did he hide his face?
Love fled and hid his face among a crowd of stars.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
The poem "When You Are Old" is written by W.B. Yeats. It describes the poet's true and sincere love for his beloved. The poet imagines his beloved in the future when she is old, with grey hair, full of sleep, and nodding by the fire, reading a book of poems written by him. As she reads the book, she remembers her youth, beauty, and the many admirers who loved her.

While others loved her beauty and graceful appearance, the poet loved her pilgrim soul and the sorrows of her changing face. He truly loved her, but she ignored his true love. In her old age, she realizes that true love has faded away and regrets not loving the poet. The poem highlights the difference between physical beauty and true love and shows the poet's everlasting love for his beloved.

The Gardener

P. Lankesh

📌 Important Questions
1. What were the old man's eyes suffused with in the story "The Gardener"?
The old man's eyes were suffused with strange memories and native intelligence.
2. Mention any two physical features of the old man standing in the coconut grove.
The old man had grey hair, a long beak-like nose, and a tall figure.
3. Mention any two changes that took place in the owner's lifestyle after the arrival of the old man, in 'The Gardener'.
The owner became lazy and stopped working hard. He also spent more time with friends.
4. Mention any two thoughts of the owner's wife on the effect of the old man's arrival on her husband's life.
She found the change strange. She worried that her husband had developed bad habits.
5. What qualities of the old man improved the income of the garden?
He was skilled in agriculture and understood the workers well.
6. How much land did Tammanna and Basavaiah own when the entire land of the village belonged to either of them?
Tammanna owned 1000 acres, and Basavaiah owned 800 acres.
7. Mention any two of the ways suggested by Tammanna's supporters to get back his land from Basavaiah.
They suggested filing a lawsuit or complaining to the police.
8. What were the two things done by Tammanna to annihilate Basavaiah completely that made the competition move from the visible to the invisible?
He composed ballads about his experiences and got them sung in public.
9. What did the songs of Tammanna say about Basavaiah?
The songs spoke about Basavaiah's cruelty and meanness.
10. What did the critics do with Tammanna's songs, in 'The Gardener'?
The critics analysed and translated Tammanna's songs.
11. How did the critics earn their share of fame using Tammanna's songs in 'The Gardener'?
They earned fame by analysing and translating Tammanna's songs.
12. Though Basavaiah filled his house with material wealth, how did his house look? Why?
His house looked dull and empty because Tammanna's books were not there.
13. Mention any two things that Basavaiah did after Tammanna became popular as the best poet of his time.
He invited scholars and poets to his house to gain popularity.
14. Name any two reasons that made Tammanna think of killing himself in "The Gardener".
He wanted to punish Basavaiah and destroy his rivalry.
15. Mention the names of the two countries whose enmity is discussed in the story "The Gardener".
The two countries whose enmity is discussed are Russia and America.
16. What happened to Tammanna after the death of Basavaiah in 'The Gardener'?
After Basavaiah's death, Tammanna forgot all his songs and became a non-entity.
17. Who paints well, according to the old man? What was he suffering from in the story 'The Gardener'?
According to the old man, Lokya's son painted well. He was suffering from fever.
📘 Additional Questions
18. What were the two things that the old man was holding when the narrator noticed him standing in a coconut grove near Chennarayapatna?
The old man was holding a spade and a newspaper tucked under his arm.
19. What happened to Basavaiah after Tammanna gave up everything and went away? Why?
Basavaiah died a few days later because he had no rival left.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
"The Gardener" is a short story by P. Lankesh which tells us about rivalry and its consequences. An old man got a job as a gardener in a coconut farm near Channarayapatna after walking several hundred miles. He was very intelligent, knew a lot about agriculture, and understood the problems of the workers. When he stopped the small thefts in the farm, the owner's income increased. As the owner became rich, he became lazy, picked up bad habits, and started spending money carelessly, which worried his wife.

The gardener tried to comfort her by telling the story of Tammanna and Basavaiah. Tammanna had one thousand acres of land, while Basavaiah had only eight hundred acres. Basavaiah asked Tammanna to sell two hundred acres of his land, but Tammanna refused. Basavaiah was mad with rage and took Tammanna's land by force. Instead of going to the court or the police, Tammanna got an idea of writing and singing ballads about his achievements. He became very famous, and people everywhere came to know about him.

But Basavaiah could not do this. Instead, he went on collecting more money, land, and property. One day, Tammanna fell ill, and Basavaiah thought that his rival would soon die. However, Tammanna wanted to defeat Basavaiah in a different way. He gave up everything and left the village, leaving Basavaiah without a rival.

Basavaiah soon found life dull and without purpose. He could not live without competition and died soon after. The story shows that rivalry and unhealthy competition can destroy a person's peace of mind. It also conveys the message that a person must adjust to situations; otherwise, he cannot survive in the world.

To The Foot From Its Child

Pablo Neruda

📌 Important Questions
1. What would the foot like to be? OR Name the two wishes of the child's foot.
The child's foot would like to be a butterfly or an apple.
2. Name any two things that teach the foot that it cannot fly.
Stones, bits of glass, streets, and ladders teach the foot that it cannot fly.
3. What does time teach the foot?
Time teaches the foot that it cannot be an apple or a butterfly. It must walk on the paths of life.
4. Mention any two places where the foot walks in the shoe as a blind man.
The foot walks through fields, mines, markets, and ministries.
📘 Additional Questions
5. How has Neruda explained the changes in the soft nails?
The soft nails gradually grow hard like horn. The toes become rough and callused with time.
📖 Summary (3/4 Marks)
"To the Foot from Its Child" is a poem written by Pablo Neruda. The poet uses the word "foot" as a metaphor for children and describes the dreams and realities of life. In the beginning, the child is not aware that it is a foot and dreams of becoming a butterfly or a fruit when it grows up.

As it grows up, it faces the difficulties of life and comes to know that it cannot become a butterfly or an apple. The foot feels trapped because it has to live inside a shoe and slowly becomes rough. It gets no rest as it works continuously in fields, mines, and markets.

The foot has to work, walk, and face many challenges throughout its life. It spends its life doing its duties without achieving its dreams and remains unaware of the harsh realities of life. Even after death, it is buried underground, where there is only darkness.

Thus, the poem tells us about the life of children from birth to death and even after death. It shows how children start life with dreams and hopes but later face the realities and struggles of life.

I Believe Books Will Never Disappear

Robert Alfino with Jorge Luis Borges

📌 Important Questions
1. Who was Borges' mother? What feeling did she have towards him?
Dona Leonor was Borges' mother. She was extremely kind to him.
2. Name any two things that happen in human life that can be considered as resources, according to Borges.
Humiliations, misfortunes, and embarrassments can be considered as resources.
3. "Alles Nahe Werd Fern" - Whose words are these, and what do they mean?
These are Goethe's words. They mean "All that is near becomes far."
4. According to Borges, who made remarkable comments on books? What is the name of the book?

🅰️ Article — THE

THE — The Definite Article
We use THE for a particular, specific, definite, or unique person or thing.

This is the book I wanted. Give me the pen on the table.
When to Use THE
1. Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, island groups
the Ganga, the Nile, the Arabian Sea, the Pacific Ocean, the Himalayas, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands

2. Certain countries (plural/descriptive names)
the Netherlands, the United States, the UK, the UAE
(but: India, Japan, Brazil — no THE)

3. Certain abbreviations
the USA, the UK, the WHO, the IAS, the IPS, the IFS

4. Unique things
the Sun ☀️, the Moon 🌙, the Earth 🌍, the sky, the world

5. Superlative adjectives
the best, the tallest, the largest, the most beautiful

6. Ordinal numbers
the first, the second, the third, the last

7. Directions/regions
the North, the South, the East, the West

8. Nouns made specific by a phrase/clause
the men in blue, the school where I studied, the girl who won the prize

9. Religious books
the Bible, the Quran, the Gita

10. Geographical features
the Persian Gulf, the Bay of Bengal, the Arabian Sea
Planets — Usually NO 'The'
Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, Neptune, Uranus — no THE.
✅ Jupiter is the largest planet. ❌ The Jupiter

Special exceptions: the Earth, the Sun, the Moon
Ø Zero Article (No Article)
1. Most proper names — Ø India, Ø Priya, Ø Venus
2. Languages — I speak Ø English.
3. Subjects — I study Ø Mathematics.
4. Meals in general — I had Ø breakfast.
5. Abstract/uncountable nouns — Ø Honesty is important. I like Ø music.

⭐ Golden Rule
A preposition shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word in a sentence.

Examples: in, on, at, by, for, from, with, to, of, about, under, between, among.
1. Prepositions of Time ⏰
AT — exact times
at 6 o'clock • at noon • at night

ON — days and dates
on Monday • on Sunday morning • on 15 August

IN — months, years, seasons, parts of the day

2. Prepositions of Place 📍
IN — inside a place or area
in the room • in India • in the box

ON — on a surface

📘 Subject–Verb Agreement

IS
Use is with one person or one thing.

He is a student. She is happy. It is a beautiful day. Ramesh is my friend. The car is new. The book is on the table. My brother is at home.
ARE
Use are with more than one person or thing, and with you.

You are my friend. We are students. They are happy. Ramesh and Akash are friends. The boys are playing. The books are on the table. My parents are at home.
HAS
Use has with one person or one thing.

He has a pen. She has a book. It has four legs. Ramesh has a car. The boy has a bag. My sister has a new phone.
HAVE
Use have with I, you, and more than one person or thing.

I have a question. You have a pen. We have a class today. They have two books. Ramesh and Akash have bicycles. The students have an exam tomorrow.
WAS
Use was with one person or one thing in the past.

I was tired. He was absent. She was happy. It was cold. Ramesh was late. The boy was at school.
WERE
Use were with more than one person or thing, and with you.

You were late. We were happy. They were tired. Ramesh and Akash were absent. The students were in the classroom. The books were on the table.
⭐ Special Subject–Verb Rules
ONE OF → is / was
One of my friends is a dancer.
One of my friends was absent yesterday.

💡 The main subject is one, so use a singular verb.
THE LIST / PROBLEM OF → look at the first noun
The list of items is on the table.
The problem of the people is solved easily.

💡 List = singular → is
💡 Problem = singular → is
A NUMBER OF → plural verb
A number of students are absent.
A number of accidents were reported.
THE NUMBER OF → singular verb
The number of students is increasing.
The number of accidents was high.
EACH → singular verb
Each of the students has a book.
Each of the children was given a prize.
AND → usually plural
Ramesh and Akash are friends.
The teacher and students are in the classroom.
My brother and sister have arrived.
EITHER...OR / NEITHER...NOR
Look at the subject closest to the verb.

Either the boy or his sisters have broken the tray.
Either the sisters or the boy has broken the tray.
AS WELL AS
The verb agrees with the first subject.

Kindness as well as justice requires this.
The teacher as well as the students is ready.

Passage 1 — The Domestic Cat
The story of the domestic cat's relationship with humans is an interesting tale. The cat has been the object of adoration, reverence, hatred and even persecution by humans throughout time. Cats were first domesticated by the ancient Egyptians as early as 3000 BC. African wild cats started preying upon the mice and rats that filled the Egyptian grain stores, and it didn't take long for the Egyptians to become appreciative of the cats' help in eliminating the rodent population. Killing a cat, even when accidental, was punishable by death. Egyptians shaved away their eyebrows as a symbol of grief when their pet passed away; they would even mummify the cat and bury it in a special cemetery, with supplies of mummified rats for the afterlife.

From Egypt, cats spread to other parts of the world. They were a great success in the East, where they were again thought to have magical and mystical qualities. Artists in China and Japan celebrated these animals in their art. In Japan, cats are seen as lucky. One of the most known is the beckoning cat, often regarded as a good luck charm for both households and businesses.

The cat spread across Europe during the Roman Empire. The Romans kept the animals to be petted and for companionship, as well as for controlling the rat and mouse population. Cats were represented mainly as working animals in Roman art, and there is little indication of reverence or mystical powers given to them.

During the Middle Ages, however, cats became an object of superstition and were associated with evil. They were often believed to be endowed with powers of black magic and suspected of being owned and used by witches. As a result, cats were beaten, killed and driven away from towns and villages. The destruction of cats was so extensive that disease-carrying rats flourished, contributing greatly to the widespread epidemics and plagues throughout Europe.

Not surprisingly, the Europeans once more began to realize the value of cats in eliminating rodents, and cats gradually regained acceptance as household pets.

Passage 3 — How a Newspaper is Made
Newspapers start the day for many of us. To begin with, the members of the newspaper staff, including the editor, make plans for the contents of the next edition of the newspaper during their daily or weekly editorial meetings. Journalists then carry out research to gather all the facts that relate to the story they have been assigned. Besides collecting information from books, magazines, other newspapers and the internet, journalists also conduct interviews with people involved in the issue. After the story is written, the editor decides whether the angle of the story is correct and whether the story is newsworthy. Once the story is approved by the editor, it goes to the copy desk.

A copy editor checks the story for grammatical errors and misspellings, and makes the story easier to read. The copy editor looks for holes or gaps in the story that might leave readers with too many unanswered questions and cause miscommunication. If the story or article is too long, it is edited down to the right size, and an eye-catching headline is added. Illustrators are responsible for creating charts and illustrations that certain articles need.

The articles, photographs and advertisements are laid out on each page of the newspaper. Page layouts are done by computer using special software for designing pages. The finished layout of the newspaper is transferred electronically from computers to the printing press. After printing, the newspapers are bundled into groups, loaded into trucks and sent out to be delivered.

✏️ Passive Voice

⭐ Q11 • 3 Marks • Asked Every Year

What is Passive Voice?
Passive Voice is used when the action is more important than the doer.

✅ Active: She writes a letter.
✅ Passive: A letter is written by her.
Formula
📗 Present: is / am / are + V³
Example: The letter is written.

📙 Past: was / were + V³
Example: The house was built.

📘 Future: will be + V³
Example: The work will be completed.

📕 Modal: can / could / should / may / might / must + be + V³
Example: The work can be done.

Steps to Change Direct Speech into Reported Speech
1. Remove commas and quotation marks (" ").
2. Use that between the reporting and reported speech (only for statements).
3. Change the first letter to small (except I).
4. Change the pronouns (I, We, You, My, etc.).
5. Change the tense of the verb.
6. Change time and place words.

Statements — use "said that"
DS: She said, "I am happy."
RS: She said that she was happy.
WH Questions — use "asked + WH word"
DS: He said, "Where are you going?"
RS: He asked where I was going.
Yes / No Questions — use "asked if/whether"
DS: She said, "Are you ready?"
RS: She asked if I was ready.
Commands / Requests — use "told / requested / ordered + to"
DS: Father said, "Study well."
RS: Father told me to study well.
Negative Commands — use "told / warned + not to"
DS: Mother said, "Do not waste time."
RS: Mother warned me not to waste time.

📝 Note Making

⭐ Q33 • 4 × ½ = 2 Marks

What is Note Making?
Note making is the process of writing the main ideas of a passage in a short and organized form. It helps us understand and remember the important points easily.
Steps to Answer
1. Read the passage carefully.
2. Identify the main topic.
3. Find the important points.
4. Fill the boxes with suitable keywords or short phrases.
5. Follow the order of the passage.
6. Avoid writing full sentences.
7. Check your spelling before submitting.
Important Tips
✅ Write only keywords.
✅ Do not copy complete sentences.
✅ Read the passage twice.
✅ Understand the relationship between the boxes.
✅ Write neat and correct spellings.
📌 Remember
Read → Understand → Identify → Fill the boxes.
Use only the required word or phrase.
Keep your answers short and meaningful.
